Warning Signs of Crisis

Immediate Warning Signs

  • Talking about wanting to die or to kill oneself
  • Looking for a way to kill oneself
  • Talking about feeling hopeless or having no reason to live
  • Talking about feeling trapped or in unbearable pain
  • Talking about being a burden to others
  • Increasing use of alcohol or drugs
  • Acting anxious or agitated; behaving recklessly
  • Sleeping too little or too much
  • Withdrawing or feeling isolated
  • Showing rage or talking about seeking revenge
  • Displaying extreme mood swings

What to Do

  1. Do not leave the person alone - Stay with them until help arrives
  2. Remove any means of self-harm - Firearms, medications, sharp objects
  3. Call 988 or 911 immediately - Don't wait to see if they get better
  4. Listen without judgment - Show you care and take them seriously
  5. Get professional help - Call emergency services or take them to ER

Create a Safety Plan

A safety plan is a prioritized list of coping strategies and sources of support to help you during a mental health crisis. Having a plan in place can help you stay safe and get the help you need.

1

Recognize Warning Signs

Identify your personal warning signs that a crisis may be developing.

2

Internal Coping Strategies

List activities you can do by yourself to take your mind off problems.

3

Social Contacts

Identify friends, family, or professionals who can help distract you.

4

Professionals & Agencies

List mental health professionals and crisis hotlines you can contact.

5

Secure Environment

Make your environment safe by removing means of self-harm.
